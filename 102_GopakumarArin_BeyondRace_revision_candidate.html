IB 35AC Creative Project | Human Biological Variation

Race is not a biological category.

Human variation is real. It just does not divide cleanly into races. It appears as gradients, local adaptations, and overlapping patterns shaped by migration, environment, and history.

Audience: This site is written for college students and general readers who have heard that “race is biological,” but may not know how geneticists and biological anthropologists actually study human variation.

Arin Gopakumar | Section 102 | May 2026

1.The misconception

In the 1700s, European naturalists sorted humanity into a few supposed “races.” That older classification treated humans as if they could be divided like separate species. Modern genetics does not support that picture.

The stronger lesson is not that human difference is imaginary. It is that the old racial boxes are too crude to describe it. A better biological explanation asks where traits change gradually, which environmental pressures shaped them, and how much variation exists inside any population.

Race is socially powerful, but it is a poor biological map of human diversity.
Claim

Race divides humans into natural biological groups.

Evidence

Most variation is within populations, and visible traits do not all share the same map.

Better frame

Use ancestry, population history, environment, and specific traits.

2.Clines, not categories

A cline is a gradual change in a trait across geography. Skin pigmentation is the clearest example because it changes with ultraviolet radiation. Darker pigmentation is common near the equator, while lighter pigmentation is more common at higher latitudes.

Race would predict that many traits should sort into the same fixed groups. They do not. Skin color, blood type, lactase persistence, and sickle-cell frequency each follow different patterns.

3.Adaptation, not race

Many population differences make more sense as local adaptations than as racial markers. The same pressure can produce similar outcomes in groups with different histories.

UV radiation

Skin pigmentation

Melanin protects folate in high-UV environments. Lower UV environments favor lighter pigmentation for vitamin D synthesis.

Temperature

Body shape

Body proportions can reflect heat conservation or heat release. This tracks climate better than racial labels.

Dairy herding

Lactase persistence

Adult milk digestion evolved independently in Northern Europe, East Africa, and the Arabian Peninsula.

Malaria pressure

Sickle-cell allele

The allele is elevated in regions where malaria has been common, including parts of Africa, the Mediterranean, the Middle East, and South Asia.

4.Where variation actually lives

Lewontin’s classic study asked how human genetic variation is distributed. The key finding is still useful for students: most genetic variation is not between “races,” but inside local populations.

~85%
of human genetic variation is found within local populations, not between the broad groups usually called races.

5.Why this matters

Rejecting biological race does not mean ignoring racism. Graves’s point is important: social categories can still affect bodies through unequal housing, health care, pollution exposure, stress, and wealth.

So the goal is not to erase human difference. The goal is to describe difference more accurately. Biology gives us gradients, adaptations, ancestry, migration, and population history. Race gives us a blunt social label that often hides the real explanation.

Takeaway: Human variation is real, but racial categories are not the biological structure of that variation.
